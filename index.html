INDX Cold Pull

Generates a guided cold-pull procedure for the Prusa CORE One INDX, to clear a partially clogged nozzle. Prusa's built-in wizard (M1702) is not enabled for INDX, so there is no in-firmware equivalent.

Do these at the printer first.

Most are menu-only settings that can neither be read nor set over G-code, so nothing here can check them for you, and each fails silently rather than loudly. The filament-type item is the exception: that one is a change the procedure makes to your printer's saved settings, and you need to know it is coming.

What it does

Pick toolHeating requires a latched tool, so the pick must come first.
Flush~60 mm of the selected cleaning filament at the flush temperature to displace old material.
PackSmall extrusions while cooling toward the material profile's packing temperature, so the plug casts the nozzle interior.
Deep coolFan-assisted to the material profile's deep-cool temperature with a fixed dwell, so the plug grips the debris.
PullReheat to the pull temperature in two stages to avoid overshoot, raise motor current, and retract 100 mm hard.
RestoreUndo every session change: motor current, extrusion guard, stall detection — and, over USB Serial, the filament type.
Wipe & dockRewarm the nozzle so residue releases instead of dragging cold strings, then dock automatically — no manual parking. The USB-file route also gets the firmware's end-of-print wastebin wipe, warm.

Prompts appear on the printer's screen and wait for a knob press — six in all: confirm setup, insert the selected cleaning filament (not before), start the purge (from here, watch the nozzle tip for melted filament flowing out), confirm what came out, start the pull, remove the strand. Success looks like three thin strands with visible dark debris; repeat until the tip comes out clean, then refit the PTFE tube and put the nozzle's filament type back.

Verified against Prusa-Firmware-Buddy 6.6.3+15625 and 6.9.0. Use at your own risk — this drives a hot nozzle and a high-current motor.
